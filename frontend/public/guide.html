The Fundamental Flaw

Why Naive "AI + AIS" Hackathon Solutions Fail Operationally

Most software projects treat oil spill detection as a simple computer vision task: detect an oil polygon on satellite imagery, find the nearest vessel on an AIS map, and accuse that ship of being the polluter. In the real world, this is scientifically invalid and legally indefensible.

❌ What Naive Projects Do
  • The Nearest-Ship Fallacy: They pick whatever ship is currently closest to the slick when the satellite flies overhead.
  • Ignores Ocean Physics: Wind and surface currents move slicks at 2%–4% of wind speed plus ocean currents. By the time a satellite observes the slick (2–8 hours later), it has drifted 10–50 kilometers away.
  • Forced Accusation: If 3 ships are in the area, their model forcibly picks the highest score as the culprit.
  • Legally Dangerous: Accuses innocent merchant ships that were merely sailing past an old slick.
✅ What NAUTRACE Does
  • Physical Backward Hindcasting: We reverse-track 500 virtual oil particles backward in time using Runge-Kutta 4th Order numerical integration with real ocean currents and Stokes wave drift.
  • Probabilistic Origin Envelopes: Instead of a single fake point, we calculate 50% and 90% Mahalanobis origin ellipses.
  • Unknown / Non-AIS Hypothesis: If no ship matches the physical release window, the system explicitly attributes to UNKNOWN_NON_AIS, recognizing that polluters often turn off their AIS transponders (IMO Resolution A.1106).
  • Evidence-Grade Provenance: Computes cryptographic SHA-256 hashes of the satellite scene, algorithm config, and met-ocean forcing for court admissibility.

Scientific Truth

Real-World Operational Failure Modes & Mitigations

To maintain absolute scientific integrity, we document the physical failure modes of maritime satellite systems and how Nautrace addresses each one.

1. SAR Low-Wind Look-Alikes (< 3 m/s)

The Problem: When surface winds are below ~3 m/s, the sea surface becomes mirror-smooth. No capillary waves form, so radar reflects away from the satellite, creating large dark patches that look identical to oil slicks.
Nautrace Mitigation: The pipeline pulls ECMWF wind forcing. If wind is below 3 m/s, the system flags the detection as "High Look-Alike Risk / Unreliable".

2. The Unknown Spill Age Dilemma

The Problem: A satellite image only gives the time of observation (t_obs). It does not timestamp when the valve was opened. Was the oil dumped 2 hours ago or 10 hours ago?
Nautrace Mitigation: Instead of guessing a single age, we apply an empirical release-time prior window (e.g. 2 to 6 hours prior to acquisition) and sample it stochastically across the ensemble.

3. Coarse Ocean Current Resolution (1/12°)

The Problem: Global ocean circulation models (CMEMS SMOC) have a 1/12° grid resolution (~9 km). Near ports and coastlines, bathymetry, rip tides, and harbor currents occur at sub-kilometer scales.
Nautrace Mitigation: We combine general circulation with Stokes wave drift and add horizontal Brownian dispersion (Kh = 10 m²/s) to account for unresolved sub-grid turbulence.

4. Intentional AIS Blackouts & "Dark Vessels"

The Problem: Ships dumping oily bilge water often switch off their AIS transponder 15 minutes prior to discharge.
Nautrace Mitigation: By maintaining an explicit UNKNOWN_NON_AIS hypothesis in our softmax competition, the system refuses to convict innocent vessels simply because their transponders were on, directly adhering to IMO Resolution A.1106(29).
